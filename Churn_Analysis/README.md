
# Customer Churn Analysis — Excel Dashboard

## Project Summary

Analysis of <b>6,688</b> telecommunications customer records to identify churn drivers, quantify revenue impact, and recommend retention strategies. Built in Excel using pivot tables, pivot charts, and KPI calculations, with an accompanying stakeholder presentation deck.
<p>
<br>

## Results at a Glance
<ul>
<li><b>26.9% churn rate</b> — 1,796 of 6,688 customers churned, representing an estimated $668K in lost annual revenue</li>
<li><b>288% of churned customers</b> were on month-to-month contracts, making contract flexibility the single strongest churn predictor in the dataset</li>
<li><b>$620K+ in lost revenue</b> tied to competitor-related churn — the largest single driver of departures</li>
<li>Customer service call volume showed <b>no meaningful correlation with churn</b> (~4% difference between high- and low-contact customers) ruling out a commonly assumed cause</li>
</ul>
<br>

## Business Questions

This project focuses on answering several key questions commonly faced by business leaders:

<ul>
<li>Which customers are most likely to churn and why? </li>
<li>Are contract type and payment behavior linked to higher churn?</li>
<li>How much revenue is churn actually costing the business?</li>
<li>What retention strategies would have the highest impact?</li>
<li>What actions could help reduce churn and improve retention?</li>
</ul>

## Key Findings

<b>Contract type is the dominant churn driver.</b> Month-to-month customers accounted for 88% of all churn — customers without a long-term commitment are far more likely to leave. This single factor outweighs every other variable in the dataset.

<b>Competitive pressure, not service quality, drives most churn.</b> Competitor-related departures accounted for over $620K in lost revenue. By contrast, customer service call volume showed almost no correlation with churn (~4% difference between high- and low-contact groups) — ruling out the common assumption that support experience is the primary churn driver.

<b>Churn is concentrated, not evenly distributed.</B> Gender showed no meaningful difference in churn rate (1.2%), meaning retention efforts are far better targeted at contract type and competitive positioning than demographic segmentation.
<p></p>
<br>

## Strategic Recommendations
<ol>
<li><b>Convert month-to-month customers to longer-term contracts.</li></b>b> This can be done through loyalty incentives or bundled discounts; the highest-leverage lever in the dataset.</li>
<li><b>Address competitive pricing pressure directly.</b> Evaluate pricing and promotional strategy against competitor offers, since this is the largest single revenue driver of churn.</li>
<li><b>Deprioritize support-experience initiatives as a churn fix.</b> The data doesn't support it as a primary driver, though it may still be worth pursuing for other reasons.</li>
<li><b>Build targeted outreach for high-risk segments.</b> These are identified through contract type and payment behavior, rather than broad-based retention campaigns.</li>
</ol>
